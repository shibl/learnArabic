# 02 · Market

## Size of the audience
The Arab diaspora outside the Arab world is commonly estimated at **30–50 million people** ([Wikipedia: Arab diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_diaspora)). Figures are rough and from different years, so we treat them as orders of magnitude.

| Host country | Estimate (people of Arab origin) | Fit for Huroof v1 |
|---|---|---|
| France | 5.5–7 M | **High** — recent generations, strong Maghrebi Arabic at home, French UI needed |
| United States | ~3.5–3.7 M | **High** — English UI, many recent arrivals |
| Germany | ~1.4 M | **High** — large recent Syrian/Iraqi arrivals, many young children |
| Canada | ~0.75 M | **High** — English + French UI, fast-growing community |
| Sweden | ~0.54 M | Medium — many young families; Swedish UI later |
| United Kingdom | ~0.5 M | High — English UI |
| Australia | ~0.5 M | High — English UI |
| Brazil / Argentina | multi-million, mostly 3rd+ generation | Low for v1 — Arabic rarely spoken at home |

**Our real target is narrower:** families where at least one parent speaks Arabic at home and who have children aged 4–10. A conservative working assumption:

- Arabic-speaking-at-home diaspora in our first-wave countries (FR, US, DE, CA, UK, AU, SE): ~8–10 M people
- ~10% are children aged 4–10 → **~0.8–1 M children**
- If 30% of those families actively want help with Arabic reading → **~250–300k families** reachable addressable market in the first wave.

At ~$50/year per family, that's a **~$12–15 M/year** serviceable market before schools — enough for a sustainable, focused business, not a venture-scale one on families alone. Weekend schools and community centres expand it (see business model).

## Trends working for us
- Large recent migration waves (2011 onwards) have produced a generation of young children born abroad to Arabic-speaking parents.
- Parents increasingly accept screen time if it is educational and bounded.
- Weekend Arabic schools exist in most cities but run once a week and look for homework tools.

## Assumptions to validate (with real families)
1. Parents will pay for reading skills, not just "exposure."
2. Kids aged 4–6 can do daily 5-minute sessions without a parent sitting with them.
3. Interface in the local language is preferred over all-Arabic.
4. Dialect bridging (home word → standard word) is valued, not confusing.
