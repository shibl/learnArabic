# 05 · Personas

## Child personas
### Yusuf, 5 — "the listener"
Born in Toronto. Understands his mother's Syrian Arabic, answers in English. Can't read. Loves dinosaurs, tablets and stickers. Attention span: 5–7 minutes.
**Needs:** big buttons, audio for every instruction, instant rewards, no reading required to navigate.

### Lina, 8 — "the reluctant reader"
Born in Lyon, Algerian parents. Goes to Saturday Arabic school, finds it boring and hard. Knows most letters in isolation, struggles with joined forms and short vowels.
**Needs:** feeling of progress, games that aren't "babyish", connecting her home words (darija) to what she reads.

### Adam & Sara, 7 and 4 — "siblings on one tablet"
**Needs:** separate profiles, quick switching, a mode to play together.

## Parent personas
### Rania, 36 — "the busy guilt-ridden parent"
Nurse in Berlin, Iraqi. Speaks Arabic to the kids but they reply in German. Reads Arabic fine but has no time to teach.
**Needs:** something the kids do alone, a weekly summary, a 1-minute thing she can do with them at bedtime.

### Karim, 40 — "the rusty reader"
Engineer in Montreal, Moroccan, left school in Arabic at 12. Speaks darija, reads slowly, unsure of grammar.
**Needs:** not to be embarrassed; clear guidance; the app also helps him refresh.

## Teacher persona
### Ustadha Huda, 45 — weekend school teacher
Teaches 20 kids of mixed levels on Saturdays in London.
**Needs:** assign practice between classes, see who practised, a level placement for new kids.

## Jobs to be done
- *When my child answers me in English, I want them to keep Arabic alive so they can talk with family back home.*
- *When we visit grandparents, I want my child to read a sign or a short story and feel proud.*
- *When I drop my child at weekend school, I want them to practise during the week without me nagging.*
